Emerging Themes

Patterns that recurred across many independent interviews, not just one voice.

Accuracy is non-negotiable

80% of interviewees ranked accuracy as a non-negotiable requirement — several cited past experience with inaccurate home INR monitors that eroded clinical trust entirely.

Continuous > periodic, especially for clinicians

65% of all interviewees wanted a continuous signal over a periodic one; 100% of cardiac surgeons/anesthesiologists specifically confirmed this.

Workflow/EMR integration is the real adoption gate — not cost

61% named workflow and EMR integration as the critical adoption factor; only 32% raised cost, and most of those were industry contacts, not clinicians.

DOAC home monitoring is a genuine "blue ocean"

Zero of 47 interviewees could name any approved home monitoring product for a DOAC (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban) — a true white-space opportunity, longer-term.

A physician champion is the real unlock

Multiple hospital product-development leaders and investors independently stressed that a named, engaged physician champion inside the target hospital — not the technology alone — determines whether a first pilot happens.

The FDA pathway is harder than founders expect

Several contacts with direct device-commercialization experience warned the regulatory pathway is the single hardest part of the roadmap, and that referencing an existing predicate device (510(k)) is far faster than a genuinely novel pathway (De Novo).

What We Assumed vs. What We Discovered

Assumed: home/warfarin patients were the beachhead
Discovered: cardiac surgeons said "I need this in my OR right now." ~500,000 CPB procedures/yr in the U.S., every one with a monitoring blind spot. Beachhead corrected to cardiac surgery, then ECMO/ICU, then interventional cath lab.
Assumed: cost was the #1 adoption barrier
Discovered: workflow and EMR integration is the real barrier. Cost was rarely raised by clinicians — mostly by industry contacts.
Assumed: strong science alone would drive smooth adoption
Discovered: a named physician champion inside the target hospital is the actual precondition for a first pilot — which is why Dr. Khawaja's role as Clinical Collaborator, not just an advisor, is central to our plan.
Assumed: DOACs would be a hard market to break into
Discovered: zero approved home DOAC tests exist anywhere. 6 million U.S. patients, no competitor to displace — a long-term "blue ocean," not a competitive fight.
