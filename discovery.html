Hypotheses Tested

Core assumptions tested through customer interviews, with the specific evidence behind each status.

#HypothesisSegmentStatusEvidence
H1 Cardiac surgeons and perfusionists experience real monitoring gaps from intermittent ACT testing Cardiac Surgery Validated Cardarelli, Toloueitabar, Ravikant, and Thakur independently confirmed ACT's failure modes and the value of continuous data.
H2 ECMO specialists would adopt a continuous monitor that eliminates hourly ACT draws ECMO / ICU Validated Cardarelli named ECMO the single highest-priority market; Toloueitabar confirmed value in ECMO/dialysis; Kroh confirmed unmet need in critical/sepsis cases.
H3 DOAC patients have no reliable home monitoring option and want one Home / DOAC Validated, with caveat Confirmed zero DOAC home-monitoring option exists, a real opening. But Gioffre (Roche) confirmed the broader home/point-of-care coagulation market is shrinking as warfarin gives way to generic DOACs, so this is a narrow, durable opportunity rather than a broad one.
H4 Cardiac anesthesiologists, not surgeons, are the primary decision-makers for OR coagulation devices Cardiac Surgery Revised Hanna and Mango both independently said operational and product-development leaders, not any clinician, control pricing and purchasing; clinicians drive clinical adoption, not the buying decision.
H5 Hospitals would pay a premium for a device that eliminates per-test reagent costs Hospital / Admin Partially validated Cost elimination is repeatedly valued (Castro, Fisher, Bobek, Mullen, Panganiban), but under DRG bundle payment pressure (Mango) hospitals want cost parity or reduction, not a premium.
H6 Workflow and EMR integration matter more to adoption than raw device performance Hospital / Admin Validated Raised unprompted and independently by Wright, Logue, Khouy, Pierson, Shalev, and Murray, among others; the most repeated theme across all 59 interviews.
H7 The FDA regulatory pathway is the hardest, most underestimated part of commercialization Hospital / Admin Validated Klang (from direct personal FDA experience), Penoyer, Singh, Ravikant, and Murray all independently flagged this as the critical path.
H8 A clinical champion is the make-or-break factor for landing the first hospital pilot Cardiac Surgery Validated Mango, Hanna, and Mechanic each independently named securing a physician champion as the single hardest, most important milestone.

Emerging Themes

Patterns that surfaced independently across many interviewees rather than being asked for directly.

Workflow and EMR integration beat raw performance

Named as the top adoption factor far more often than accuracy or speed. Any device that adds friction to existing clinical workflows struggles regardless of how well it performs.

Operational leaders, not physicians, control pricing

Confirmed independently by two separate Orlando Health product-development leaders: clinicians drive clinical adoption and championing, but budget-holders decide whether a hospital actually buys.

The FDA pathway is the critical path, not a formality

Raised unprompted by regulatory-experienced interviewees as the single hardest part of commercialization, more difficult and slower than founders typically expect going in.

A physician champion determines the first pilot

Multiple hospital-operations interviewees called landing that first champion, and the first pilot site behind them, the hardest milestone in the entire commercialization path.

Displacing an incumbent is harder than the opportunity looks

Diagnostics-industry contacts (Siemens, Roche, Werfen, Helena) consistently warned that hospitals satisfied with a working device resist switching risk, even for a meaningfully better one.

The DOAC home-monitoring opportunity is real but narrow

Confirmed zero existing DOAC home test, a genuine blue ocean, but the broader warfarin-era home-monitoring market is structurally shrinking, so the addressable population is smaller than the initial premise assumed.
